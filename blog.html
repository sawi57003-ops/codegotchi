Codegotchi: the 1996 pocket pet, reborn on your GitHub streak

By Fngyuan · Built with Kiro for Kiroween 2026 (Resurrection)

In 1996, millions of kids carried a tiny pet in their pocket. It ate, it played, and it died if you forgot it. Thirty years later, I rebuilt that pet for developers — and this time, it lives on your GitHub streak.

Meet Codegotchi. Byte is the pet. Every commit feeds him. Every day you ship, he grows: Egg, Baby, Child, Teen, Adult — and Golden at a 30-day streak. Skip two days and he gets sick. Skip three and he joins the graveyard, and a new egg hatches in his place. The streak that matters is the one you actually kept: the stage follows the longest streak of the current life, so a sick pet keeps its evolved form. Only death sends you back to an egg.

There is a game, too: Bug Squash, sixty seconds of squashing bugs to raise happiness. And a manual check-in for days when GitHub is unreachable — it keeps the pet alive, but it never inflates the streak. The pet reads only your public GitHub activity. No login, no token, no backend, no wallet. It is a static page.

Built with Kiro, for Kiroween 2026

This entry is my second submission to Kiroween 2026, in the Resurrection category. The build itself was spec-driven: requirements, design and tasks written as Kiro specs before a line of app code, a steering file for conventions, and a lifecycle hook that runs thirty checks against the pure state machine — fresh egg, the Golden threshold, the idle-day rules, the gap-streak rule, the fed-today rule. Thirty out of thirty pass. The sprites are hand-drawn 14x12 bitmaps in a Game Boy palette, because a pocket pet should look like one.

What I like most about the idea: the Tamagotchi was always a streak machine with a screen. Developers already have a streak — the green squares on their profile. Codegotchi just gives that streak a face, a mood, and consequences.
